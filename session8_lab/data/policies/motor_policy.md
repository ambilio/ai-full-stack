# Motor Insurance Policy — Private Car Package (Extract)

Document MOT-POL-2026. Applies to all private-car package policies issued in
2026, including POL-88231 and POL-55672. Clauses are numbered M-x.y for precise
citation in claim decisions and appeals.

## Section M-1 — Cover

M-1.1 Scope of cover: This policy covers loss of or damage to the insured
private car arising from accidental external means, fire, theft, riot, strike,
flood, storm and damage in transit, together with third-party liability as
required under the Motor Vehicles Act.

M-1.2 Policy period: Cover operates only for events occurring between the
commencement and expiry dates shown on the policy schedule. Claims arising
from events outside the policy period are not payable.

## Section M-2 — Conditions precedent to liability

M-2.1 Insured declarations: The insured must provide accurate details of the
vehicle, its registration, its usage and prior claims history at proposal.
A material misstatement voids the cover from inception.

M-2.2 Permitted use: The vehicle must be used only for social, domestic and
private purposes. Use for hire or reward, racing, pace-making, reliability
trials or speed testing is excluded from cover.

M-2.3 Licence validity: A claim is payable only if the person driving the
insured vehicle at the time of the accident held a driving licence that was
valid and effective on the date of the accident for the class of vehicle
driven. If the driver's licence had expired, or stood suspended or revoked, on
the accident date, the claim shall be rejected in full. A licence within a
renewal grace period recognised by the transport authority is treated as valid
only where the renewal is subsequently granted.

## Section M-3 — Own damage and liability

M-3.1 Own-damage assessment: Repair claims are assessed against the appointed
surveyor's report together with the authorised garage estimate. The insurer
may require re-inspection of the vehicle before approval.

M-3.2 Third-party liability: Liability for third-party bodily injury and
property damage is covered to the limits stated in the schedule and is decided
by the competent tribunal where contested.

M-3.3 Exclusions: Driving under the influence of alcohol or drugs,
consequential loss, depreciation-only claims, mechanical or electrical
breakdown, and wear and tear are excluded.

## Section M-4 — Claims procedure

M-4.1 Intimation: Accidents must be intimated to the insurer within 48 hours
of occurrence. Late intimation may proportionately prejudice the claim.

M-4.2 Repair estimate: A detailed repair estimate from a garage must be
submitted before survey; repairs must not commence until the surveyor has
inspected the vehicle, except emergency safeguarding of the vehicle.

## Section M-5 — Assessment and deductions

M-5.1 Depreciation: Admitted claims are settled after deduction of
depreciation on replaced parts at the following rates: rubber, nylon and
plastic parts, tyres, tubes and batteries — 50%; fibreglass components — 30%;
all other parts including metal parts — on a scale rising from 5% for a
vehicle aged between six months and one year to 50% for a vehicle aged over
ten years. Paint material attracts 50% depreciation on the material portion of
the painting charges. The approved amount may therefore be lower than the
claimed amount even where the claim is admitted in full.

M-5.2 Compulsory excess: The excess stated in the schedule is deducted from
every own-damage settlement.

M-5.3 Salvage: Salvage of replaced parts and of a vehicle settled as a total
loss vests with the insurer.

## Section M-6 — Decisions and appeals

M-6.1 Decision communication: Approval and rejection letters state the amount
assessed and cite the specific clauses relied upon.

M-6.2 Appeal: The insured may appeal a rejection in writing within 30 days of
the date of the decision letter; the appeal window closing date is stated in
the letter. Appeals are reviewed by an officer not involved in the original
decision.
